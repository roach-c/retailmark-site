We have sat on the other side of the table

Most supplier consultants have read about how Walmart buys. Our team spent years inside it. That is the whole difference, and it shows up in small, specific ways.

01

We know what gets a pitch rejected before it is read

A buyer's category has a limited number of slots, a modular reset date that does not move, and a stack of decks to get through. Most pitches do not lose an argument. They never start one.

  • What a buyer checks first, and what they never get to
  • Why a good product with the wrong case pack is a no
  • The questions that get asked in the room, prepared for in advance
  • When "not this year" means come back, and when it does not
02

Custom, not templated

There is no RetailMark deck with your logo dropped into it. A frozen item with 60 days of shelf life and a co-packer in Texas is a different business from a shelf stable item shipping from overseas, and a plan that does not know the difference will fall apart at the first purchase order.

  • Built around your category, your capacity and your real numbers
  • Honest about what your operation can actually support
  • Sized to what you need, since almost nobody needs all nine services
03

We speak the buyer's language

Retail runs on its own vocabulary, and using it wrong is a signal. A supplier who says "planogram" when the category says "modular" has told the room something before making a single point.

  • Data presented the way the category already presents it
  • Claims that survive being checked against the buyer's own screen
  • Clear communication in the terms the business already uses
  • Every term we use is in our retail glossary

What working with us looks like

No long onboarding, and no invoice before there is a plan worth paying for.

  1. A free strategy call. You tell us the product, the volume you can support and where you are stuck. We tell you honestly whether this is the year for it.
  2. A plan against a real calendar. Line reviews happen on fixed dates. Everything gets worked backward from the one that matters for your category.
  3. The work, in the systems your buyer uses. Forecasts, item setup, the deck. Built where they can be checked, not in a vacuum.
  4. After the yes. Getting on the shelf is the start of the problem. Replenishment, compliance and reporting are what keep you there.

Based in Bentonville, Arkansas

Ten minutes from the home office. That is not a marketing line. Buyer meetings get moved, rescheduled and occasionally happen at short notice, and being in the same town as the people making the decision is a practical advantage that a supplier in another state is paying someone to have.

It also means the trade vocabulary, the calendar and the compliance programs are not something we look up. They are the local industry.
